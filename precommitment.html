Precommitment: Strategic Self-Binding

The art of making credible threats and promises by limiting your own options

What is Precommitment?

Precommitment is a strategy in which an agent deliberately limits their own future options to make a threat or promise more credible, influencing other agents' expectations and decisions.
Reduces your flexibility to gain strategic advantage
Makes promises/threats more credible by removing option to back out
Changes the expected payoff matrix for all players
Powerful tool in game theory, economics, negotiation, and politics

The Cortés Example: Burning the Ships

In 1519, Spanish conquistador Hernán Cortés ordered his ships burned after landing in Mexico. This dramatic act of precommitment:

→ Eliminated the option of retreat for his own men

→ Signaled to the Aztecs his absolute commitment to his mission

→ Changed the expected game outcome by removing certain branches of the decision tree

Formalization in Game Theory

Transforms simultaneous games into sequential games
Can change Nash equilibria by restricting strategy space
Often converts Prisoner's Dilemma into assurance games
Effective precommitment requires:
  • Observable to other players
  • Truly irreversible (or costly to reverse)
  • Changes expected utility calculations

The Hold-up Problem

When one party makes a relationship-specific investment, they become vulnerable to opportunistic behavior from their counterpart, who can extract better terms.

Auto Parts Supplier: Builds factory next to car manufacturer to save on transportation.

Problem: Once built, car manufacturer can demand lower prices knowing supplier has limited alternatives.

Paradox: Precommitment makes the supplier vulnerable to exploitation.

Government Policy Example: Terrorism

Governments want to precommit to a "no negotiations with terrorists" policy to deter future kidnappings, but face pressure to negotiate when citizens are actually taken.
Public precommitment creates credibility, but private flexibility may be needed
Solution often involves delegation to third parties or strict legal constraints

Limitations and Risks

Loss of flexibility can be costly if circumstances change
Can lead to costly conflict if both sides precommit

Core insight: sometimes the ability to commit to a future action, even if it seems irrational in isolation, can lead to more favorable outcomes across the entire decision tree.

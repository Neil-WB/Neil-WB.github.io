deploying human agents in the wild

pico-neil et al.
abstract

we've discovered that humans have remarkable test-time compute and are able to complete surprisingly long-horizon tasks. in this page, we exploit this emergent ability to execute such diverse plans as the total obliteration of the elseverian academic order, the creation of a "human spirit" playlist that gets physically mailed to people in the form of tapes, and the redaction of an oral history of exp.camp. in all tasks, pico-neil et al direct the SOTA agent "neil" to complete complex tasks with an expected horizon of 24 hours.

key findings:
(1) humans display superior real-world grounding compared to current robotic systems
(2) the meat-substrate shows unexpected compliance when properly prompted
(3) latency remains acceptable despite the biological compute's tendency toward coffee breaks
